# Steel Recycling in India: Where the Real Opportunity Lies in 2026

Steel remains one of the world's most infinitely reusable materials, and India's growing industrial base is placing fresh urgency on how the country manages its scrap ecosystem. The real story for steel recycling in 2026 is about collection, not manufacturing.

## The Scrap Supply Gap
India consumes roughly 41 million tonnes of ferrous scrap each year but generates only about 32 million tonnes domestically, leaving a structural import gap of nearly 9 million tonnes. Only around 23% of India's crude steel currently comes from recycled scrap, compared with a global average of 32%.

## End-of-Life Vehicles: The Clearest Opportunity
India has an estimated 12 million vehicles eligible for scrappage, yet only about 350,500 were formally scrapped between August 2022 and July 2025 — far short of government targets. Full implementation of the Vehicle Scrappage Policy could unlock 9 to 12 million tonnes of additional ferrous scrap annually.

## Policy Momentum in 2026
In January 2026, the Ministry of Steel signaled plans for an updated scrap recycling guideline, discussed at the 13th International Material Recycling Conference in Jaipur, alongside calls for GST rationalisation and infrastructure funding.

## Formal vs. Informal Recycling
Industry bodies have pushed for reduced GST on scrap to level the playing field between organized recyclers and the informal sector, which continues to undercut compliant players.
